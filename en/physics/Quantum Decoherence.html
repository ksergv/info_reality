What Is Quantum Decoherence?

Quantum decoherence describes the process by which a quantum system loses its coherent superposition through interaction with its environment.

Before decoherence, a quantum system can be described as existing in multiple possible states simultaneously. Interaction with the surrounding environment suppresses this superposition, making classical behavior observable.

Modern quantum mechanics successfully describes this process and many experimental results confirm its predictions.

However, while quantum decoherence explains how quantum probabilities translate into classical observations, it does not answer the question of why exactly one particular outcome is ultimately realized.

The informational model proposed in this book introduces an additional stage preceding physical decoherence.

According to this hypothesis, the selection of a particular possibility first occurs within the informational field through a process called Information Decoherence.

In the proposed model, physical quantum decoherence is considered as an observable manifestation of a high-frequency process of information exchange between the information and physical levels of reality.

From this perspective, quantum decoherence represents the second phase of the continuous, high-frequency exchange of information between the information and physical levels. During this phase, information states manifest themselves as observable physical events.
