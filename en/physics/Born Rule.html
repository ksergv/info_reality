What Is the Born Rule?

The Born Rule is one of the fundamental principles of quantum mechanics. It determines how the mathematical description of a quantum system is converted into measurable probabilities.

According to the rule, the probability of observing a particular outcome is equal to the squared magnitude of the wave function associated with that state.

The Born Rule has been confirmed by countless experiments and is an essential part of modern quantum theory. Without it, the wave function would have no direct connection to physical measurements.

Although the rule accurately predicts experimental results, quantum mechanics accepts it as a basic postulate rather than deriving it from deeper principles.

The informational model proposed in this book offers a different perspective. It suggests that the Born Rule reflects the transition from an informational state to an observable physical event.

Within this interpretation, the probability described by the Born Rule is not a fundamental property of nature but the observable projection of an underlying informational process.

This approach does not replace the Born Rule. Instead, it attempts to explain why this mathematical relationship appears in quantum mechanics and how it may arise from a deeper informational structure.
