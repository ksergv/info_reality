Information–Quantum Channel

Abstract

Axiom: The informational level of reality is understood as a fundamental informational structure represented as the informational component of a complex quantum state and manifested through its phase structure.

This appendix considers a hypothetical mechanism connecting the informational level with its physical realization. In the proposed model, the connection between the informational and quantum levels is two-way, although asymmetric in function.

The informational structure provides the initial structure that is realized through the quantum level:

I → Q

At the same time, once phase coupling is established, an image can be exchanged in both directions between the informational level and physical reality.

The Yin–Yang interaction occurs within the quantum field, after the informational structure has been transferred to the quantum level:

Q(Yin) ↔ Q(Yang)

A communication channel is therefore required. Its function is not to carry energy, but to provide the conditions for the exchange of the informational image.

1. The information–quantum channel

The fundamental informational structure is considered a primary basis underlying coherent systems. It is not a separate physical field, but contains informational states characterized by phase.

When coherence is established between an informational state and a quantum system, phase coupling arises:

Φ_I ↔ Ψ_Q

The phase coupling creates the channel of informational interaction.

In this model:

phase = condition for coupling

image = content of the exchange

Thus, when phase coupling exists, an image can be transmitted from the informational level to physical reality and from physical reality back to the informational level.

2. Phase coupling

Let the informational state be characterized by phase φ_I and the quantum state by phase φ_Q.

The phase difference is

Δφ = φ_I − φ_Q

The condition for phase coupling is approximately

Δφ ≈ 0

When the phases are properly aligned, a stable coupling channel can exist and image exchange becomes possible.

When the phase relationship is lost, the channel weakens or disappears.

Thus, in the proposed model, phase is not the content of informational exchange. It is the condition that makes the exchange possible.

The phase therefore acts as a tuning mechanism between informational structures and quantum states.

3. The image as the content of information exchange

Phase coupling itself is not the information being exchanged. It creates the condition under which an image can pass through the channel.

An image is understood here as a structured form of information capable of manifestation at the quantum and physical levels.

The initial informational image receives a physical realization:

O_I → O_R

where O_I is the informational image and O_R is the image manifested in physical reality.

The physical reality can, in turn, return an image through the same coupled channel:

O_R → O_I

Thus, the exchange of images is two-way.

However, the two directions have different functions. The informational level provides the initial balanced image, while physical reality can return changes that arise during physical realization.

The informational level is therefore not necessarily an immutable template. Stable changes arising in physical reality may become part of an updated informational structure.

4. CI as the physical manifestation of phase coupling

In the proposed model, CI is not a separate material substance or an independent physical field.

CI is the physical manifestation of coherent phase coupling between the fundamental informational structure and a quantum state.

The process can be represented schematically as:

informational structure → phase coupling → quantum state → CI → physical manifestation

CI is therefore understood as the carrier and manifestation of a coherent phase relationship between the informational and physical levels of reality.

In the reverse direction, physical reality can also transmit an image through the same channel while phase coupling is maintained.

5. Complex amplitude and the Born rule

A quantum state is described by a complex amplitude:

Ψ = a + bi

where a and b are the real components of the complex amplitude and i² = −1.

The complex conjugate is

Ψ* = a − bi

and therefore

|Ψ|² = Ψ*Ψ = a² + b²

In standard quantum mechanics, the squared modulus of the wavefunction is related to the probability of observing the corresponding state according to the Born rule.

In the proposed model, the complex structure is additionally interpreted as a possible mathematical interface between informational structure and quantum realization.

The imaginary unit i itself remains a mathematical element of the complex-number formalism. Its interpretation as being associated with the informational level belongs to the proposed hypothesis rather than to standard quantum mechanics.

6. Coherence of the channel

The quality of informational–quantum coupling is characterized by a coherence measure C.

For complete coherence:

C = 1

If several successive levels of an informational interface are considered, the overall coherence can be represented as

C = ∏(i=1 to n) k_i

where k_i is the coefficient describing preservation of coherence between neighboring levels.

Informational decoherence can then be represented as

D = 1 − C

In this model, C characterizes the quality and stability of the informational–quantum interface rather than the coherence of the informational field itself, which is assumed to be fundamentally coherent.

At C = 0, stable informational–quantum coupling is lost.

7. Stability and evolution of the image

Physical reality can generate new or modified images. Their emergence does not mean that they are automatically incorporated into the informational structure.

If the physical image coincides with the informational image,

O_R = O_I

no correction is required and the coupling remains stable.

If a change is unstable or regressive, it is not maintained by the coupled channel and may disappear when the coupling is lost.

If a new form proves stable in physical reality, it may participate in further informational exchange and become part of an updated informational structure.

The informational field therefore does not need to perform a deliberate selection. Physical reality generates variations, while stable configurations have the possibility of becoming incorporated into the continuing informational structure.

8. Vibrational codes

In the proposed model, vibrational codes are not treated as an independent source of energy transmitted to the informational level.

They may instead be understood as low-frequency phase modulation of processes involved in informational–quantum coupling.

The relevant phase relationship is

Δφ = φ_I − φ_Q

Reducing the phase difference increases phase agreement and, according to the hypothesis, increases the coherence of the channel.

Increasing phase difference produces informational decoherence.

Thus, the primary factor in the proposed mechanism is phase organization of the informational–quantum coupling, while energy belongs to the physical quantum process that is already being realized.

9. Experimental possibility

If the proposed model corresponds to a physical process, the informational level itself would not necessarily be directly measurable.

Instead, experimental investigation would have to search for reproducible physical effects associated with changes in phase coherence and in the quality of informational–quantum coupling.

The relevant question would therefore not be whether an invisible informational field can be directly detected, but whether measurable physical processes systematically correlate with the proposed coupling conditions.

Conclusion

The proposed model can be summarized as follows:

phase tuning → coupling → image exchange → quantum realization → CI → physical manifestation

The reverse direction is also possible:

physical image → quantum state → coupled channel → informational image

The essential distinction is:

phase creates the channel; the image carries the information.

The informational level provides an initial coherent image, while physical reality can return images and stable changes arising from physical realization.

CI is interpreted here not as an additional material field, but as the physical manifestation of coherent phase coupling between the informational and quantum levels.

This is a hypothesis and is not part of the established formalism of modern quantum mechanics.
