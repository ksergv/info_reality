Quantum Collapse as a Reaction of an Information-Quantum System

What Is Quantum Collapse?

In standard quantum mechanics, measurement leads to what is known as the collapse of the wave function: a system that is described by a superposition of possible states before measurement becomes a definite state after interacting with a measuring device.

At the same time, the mathematical formalism of quantum mechanics allows the probabilities of possible outcomes to be calculated precisely, but by itself it does not provide a unified physical picture of what exactly happens to the system when new information is obtained.

In the information model of reality, collapse receives a different interpretation. It is considered not as a sudden physical destruction of the wave function, but as a reaction of the quantum structure to a change in information about the system.

A Change in Information Changes the State of the System

The proposed model is based on the concept of information and quantum elements — conventionally, information and quantum cubes — between which continuous exchange takes place.

In this view, the quantum state is not a completely independent entity. It is connected to an information structure that determines the relationships between the elements of the system.

Therefore, obtaining new information about a system does not simply mean that an external observer obtains a certain value.

Obtaining information means changing the information structure itself.

If the state of a system was described by a particular set of relationships, the emergence of new information changes these relationships. Through the existing exchange between the information and quantum levels, this change is transmitted to the quantum structure.

As a result, the quantum state is reorganized in accordance with the new information.

It is precisely this reorganization that is defined in the model presented in the book as Informational Decoherence.

High-Frequency Exchange Between the Information and Quantum Levels

A key element of this interpretation is the assumption that high-frequency exchange exists between information and quantum elements.

Due to this high-frequency exchange of information between the information and quantum levels, quantum collapse occurs instantaneously.

In this case, the information and quantum states are not independent. A change at one level causes an instantaneous reorganization at the other.

change in information

change in information relationships

        ↓

        ↓

        ↓
      collapse

Thus, collapse does not necessarily have to be regarded as a separate fundamental mechanism added to quantum mechanics.

It may be an observable consequence of the interaction between the information and quantum levels of reality.

Collapse and Superposition

In this approach, superposition can be regarded as a state in which the information structure has not yet been fixed with respect to a particular outcome. Several possible states remain compatible with the existing information.

Measurement introduces new information. After that, some of the possible information relationships become incompatible with the new state of the system, and the structure is reorganized.

Therefore, collapse can be defined as:

the transition of an information-quantum system from a set of potentially compatible states to a state consistent with the newly obtained information.

At the same time, the probabilistic nature of the outcome does not disappear. Before new information is obtained, the system allows several possible outcomes. The probabilities of these outcomes are determined by the structure of the quantum state and are described by the wave function and the Born rule.

Thus, the Born rule determines the probabilistic structure of possible outcomes, while collapse describes the reorganization of the system after specific information has emerged.

Collapse as a Change in Relationships at the Information Level

Within this model, the essential point is not the notion of "collapse" itself, but the change in the connectivity of the information system — informational decoherence.

Before measurement, the system maintains a multitude of interconnected possibilities. Measurement introduces a new constraint. This constraint propagates through the information-quantum relationships and changes the configuration of the system.

The Observer and Collapse

This approach also makes it possible to look at the role of the observer differently.

The occurrence of collapse does not necessarily require a conscious observer as a special physical entity. What is fundamental is the emergence of new information and its physical interaction with the system.

A measuring device, photon, environmental particle, or another physical object may become part of the process if the interaction results in a change in the information structure of the system.

Conscious perception of the result occurs only afterward.

Therefore, collapse is not necessarily caused by consciousness; consciousness receives an already formed result of the informational reorganization.

This makes it possible to separate the physical process of measurement from the philosophical problem of conscious observation.

An Information-Based Interpretation of Collapse

Within the proposed model, quantum collapse can be formulated as follows:

Quantum collapse is the final phase of a process that begins with informational decoherence: the reorganization of information relationships changes the structure of the information state, after which the quantum state is reorganized through high-frequency exchange with quantum elements.

Thus, collapse is preceded not by an instantaneous transition of the quantum system, but by a phase of informational reorganization. During this phase, the configuration of the information relationships that determine the state of the system changes. Only after the new information configuration has formed is this change transmitted to the quantum level.

The process can be represented as the following sequence:

change in information
        ↓
informational decoherence
        ↓
reorganization of information relationships
        ↓
formation of a new information configuration
        ↓
high-frequency exchange
        ↓
reorganization of the quantum state
        ↓
quantum collapse

In this view, informational decoherence and quantum collapse are successive phases of a single process. Decoherence occurs at the information level and changes the structure of relationships, while collapse is the manifestation of this reorganization at the quantum level.

Therefore, collapse cannot be regarded as the primary event. The primary event is the change in the information structure. The quantum reorganization follows as a reaction of the interconnected information-quantum system.

Connection with Other Concepts of the Model

  • Information Cube — the elementary unit of information describing a state.
  • Information Field — the primary structure of information relationships.
  • Coherence — the state of consistency and interconnectedness of the information structure.
  • Quantum Decoherence — the process by which an initially interconnected structure separates into more independent components.
  • Wave Function — the mathematical description of the quantum state and its possible outcomes.
  • Born Rule — the probabilistic relationship between the quantum state and the result of measurement.

Within this system of concepts, collapse occupies an intermediate position between the information and physical descriptions:

change in information → change in relationships → change in the quantum state → observable physical reality.
