What Is the Virtual Body?

This chapter proposes the hypothesis that every physical body corresponds to a virtual informational body existing within the informational field. The physical body is viewed as a continuously maintained projection or the result of a high-frequency information–quantum exchange.

According to the proposed model, a human being exists simultaneously in two interconnected forms.

The first is the physical body.

It exists in space-time and is directly observable.

The second is the virtual body.

It is a stable configuration of Information Qubits existing within the informational field.

The term "virtual" does not imply that this body is imaginary or unreal.

Rather, it is virtual only with respect to physical perception.

It cannot be observed by the senses or detected by current physical instruments.

Nevertheless, it represents the primary informational structure that determines the organization of the physical body.

Information–Quantum Exchange

The virtual body and the physical body do not exist independently.

A continuous information–quantum exchange takes place between them.

This exchange occurs through a high-frequency Yin–Yang transformation.

As a result, the physical body is continuously synchronized with its informational structure.

Therefore, the physical body is not a static object.

It is continuously regenerated as a dynamic manifestation of the virtual body.

Why This Process Cannot Be Observed

If the frequency of the information–quantum exchange is much higher than the temporal resolution of any physical process, direct observation becomes impossible.

Any measuring instrument records only the averaged result.

Therefore, the absence of a directly observable mechanism does not imply the absence of the process itself.

It may simply remain hidden because of the extremely high frequency of the informational exchange.

Coherence Between the Virtual and Physical Body

In a young organism, the coherence between the virtual body and the physical body is at its maximum.

The physical body almost perfectly corresponds to its informational structure.

Over time, however, this coherence gradually decreases.

As a result, the physical body reproduces its informational template with decreasing accuracy.

Within the proposed model, this process is considered one of the possible causes of aging.

Aging as Informational Decoherence

Modern biology explains aging through the accumulation of molecular damage.

The proposed hypothesis does not reject these mechanisms.

Instead, it interprets them as physical manifestations of a deeper process.

According to this model, aging represents a gradual informational decoherence between the virtual and physical body.

Mutations, epigenetic changes, cellular aging, and other well-known biological processes are viewed as consequences of this loss of coherence.

Regeneration

If coherence between the physical body and the virtual body can be restored locally, it becomes possible to recover the original informational structure of tissues.

From this perspective, regeneration may be understood as the process of resynchronizing the physical body with its virtual informational prototype.
