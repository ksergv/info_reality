Informational Decoherence and Time

Introduction

Informational decoherence is usually considered as a loss of coherence between components of a quantum system. In the interpretation proposed here, its role is much broader. Decoherence represents a change in the degree to which the informational component participates in the realization of a system's probabilistic structure.

If the informational component is connected with the formation of probabilities, images, and their subsequent physical realization, then a change in this component should also affect the dynamics of processes.

This makes it possible to consider time not as an independent physical entity, but as a relation between the rates of different processes.

1. Time as a Relation Between Processes

Absolute time, understood as a separate physical entity, may not exist. What we call time is determined by comparing processes.

Atomic clocks do not create time; they provide a physical reference process against which the rates of other processes are measured.

In schematic form:

\[ t \sim \frac{\text{reference process}}{\text{process under consideration}}. \]

If the rate of a particular process decreases relative to the reference process, we say that time has slowed down for that process.

2. The Informational Component of a Process

In the proposed model, physical realization arises through the interaction of an informational component with a probabilistic structure.

The informational component determines an image of a possible realization, while probability determines the degree to which that image can be realized.

\[ \text{information} \rightarrow \text{image} \rightarrow \text{probability} \rightarrow \text{energetic realization}. \]

Information does not necessarily disappear through decoherence. It may continue to exist within the overall structure, while its contribution to a particular process becomes smaller.

A loss of accessible informational contribution is not equivalent to the destruction of information.

3. What Decoherence Does

In a coherent state, the different components of a system are coordinated with one another. The informational component can fully participate in the formation of probabilistic and energetic dynamics.

With decoherence, some correlations become inaccessible to the subsystem under consideration. The information itself need not disappear, but its effective contribution to a particular process is reduced.

\[ I_{\mathrm{total}} = I_{\mathrm{accessible}} + I_{\mathrm{inaccessible}}. \]

With decoherence:

\[ I_{\mathrm{accessible}}\downarrow. \]

Part of the informational-energy structure may become distributed among other degrees of freedom. If the rate of a process depends on its accessible informational-energy contribution,

\[ v=f(I,E,P), \]

then a reduction of the accessible contribution leads to

\[ v\rightarrow v', \qquad v' < v. \]

Relative to an unchanged reference process, this appears as a slowing of time.

4. Why Not All Processes Slow Down

This does not mean that decoherence must slow down all processes equally.

The slowing occurs where the informational contribution determining the corresponding probabilistic and energetic dynamics has changed. One process may lose a significant part of its informational-energy coherence, while another may remain almost unchanged.

Therefore, what matters is the relative change in the rates of processes.

\[ v_{\mathrm{process}} < v_{\mathrm{atomic}}. \]

This is the physical content of the statement that time has slowed down for the particular process.

5. The Role of Probabilities

In this interpretation, the diagonal elements of the density matrix can be regarded not only as mathematical probabilities of states, but also as the result of the informational contribution being realized within the probabilistic structure.

With decoherence, information continues to participate in the formation of probabilities, but its contribution may decrease:

\[ I\rightarrow I', \qquad I' < I. \]

Correspondingly:

\[ P_i\rightarrow P_i'. \]

The main probabilistic structure may nevertheless remain, even though the degree of informational participation in its realization is reduced.

6. From Slowing Down to Stopping

If the informational contribution becomes sufficiently small, the rate of the process may approach zero:

\[ I_{\mathrm{accessible}}\rightarrow 0, \]
\[ v_{\mathrm{process}}\rightarrow 0. \]

In this limiting case, the development of the system stops relative to the chosen reference process.

This is no longer merely a slowing of time. It is a state in which a particular process ceases to develop in its previous direction.

7. The Extreme Form of Decoherence — Reversal of a Process

If decoherence does not merely reduce the informational contribution but distorts the correspondence between information and its image, the process may not stop. Its direction may change.

Let \(D\) denote some parameter characterizing the development of a system. Then:

\[ \frac{dD}{dt}>0 \]

corresponds to development,

\[ \frac{dD}{dt}=0 \]

to stopping, and

\[ \frac{dD}{dt}<0 \]

to degradation.

In this case, decoherence acquires not only a quantitative but also a qualitative character: it becomes an inversion of the informational structure.

8. Image and Its Distortion

The informational component generates an image. The image is physically realized to the extent that it is not suppressed or distorted by other components of the probabilistic structure.

\[ \text{information} \rightarrow \text{image} \rightarrow \text{realization}. \]

In ordinary decoherence, the image becomes weaker. With stronger decoherence, it becomes distorted. In the extreme form of decoherence, an image opposite to the original informational content may arise.

This is no longer simply a loss of information, but a disruption of the correspondence between information and its realization.

9. Orwellian Inversion as an Extreme Example

A metaphorical example of such an inversion is the Orwellian picture of reality in which fundamental concepts are systematically reversed.

If peace comes to mean war, freedom means slavery, and truth is defined as the opposite of facts, the informational structure is not merely weakened. It continues to function, but its correspondence with the original meaning has been disrupted.

Within the proposed model, this can be regarded as an extreme form of informational decoherence:

\[ \text{information} \rightarrow \text{distorted image} \rightarrow \text{distorted realization}. \]

If such a structure becomes stable, the process of development may not merely stop but turn into degradation.

10. Return to Coherence

If decoherence destroys or distorts the connections between information and image, then restoration of coherence should restore those connections.

\[ \text{decoherence} \rightarrow \text{distortion} \]

and then:

\[ \text{recoherence} \rightarrow \text{restoration of connections} \rightarrow \text{restoration of the image}. \]

Thus, a return to coherence can be understood not merely as a restoration of correlations, but as a return of the system to its original informational configuration.

11. Time as a Manifestation of Informational Dynamics

In this approach, time becomes a secondary concept. What is fundamental is not the “flow of time,” but the change of states.

The rate of these changes is determined by the accessible informational-energy structure.

\[ \text{informational structure} \rightarrow \text{probabilistic structure} \rightarrow \text{dynamics} \rightarrow \text{relative rate} \rightarrow \text{time}. \]

Decoherence can act on this process at the level of informational structure.

\[ \boxed{ \begin{array}{ccc} \text{coherence} &\rightarrow& \text{development}\\ \text{decoherence} &\rightarrow& \text{slowing}\\ \text{extreme decoherence} &\rightarrow& \text{stopping or degradation} \end{array}} \]

Conclusion

In this model, decoherence is a much more fundamental phenomenon than a simple loss of quantum coherence.

It represents a change in the degree of connection between information, probability, energy, and physical realization.

If the accessible informational-energy contribution decreases, the rate of the corresponding process decreases relative to other processes. This manifests itself as a slowing of time.

If the contribution becomes insufficient, the process stops.

If the correspondence between information and its image itself is disrupted, the direction of the process may change, and development turns into degradation.

\[ \boxed{ \text{coherence} \rightarrow \text{slowing} \rightarrow \text{stopping} \rightarrow \text{inversion} } \]

Restoration of coherence represents the reverse process: restoration of informational connections, the image, and, in the limiting case, the original direction of development.
