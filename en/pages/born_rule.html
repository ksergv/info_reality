What Is the Born Rule?

The Born Rule

In modern quantum mechanics, probability is determined by the squared magnitude of the wave function.

This rule has been confirmed experimentally with remarkable accuracy, yet it is accepted as a fundamental postulate.

Within the proposed model, the Born rule can be interpreted differently.

The operation of taking the squared magnitude represents the transition from an informational state to a physically observable result.

In other words, physical probability emerges as a projection of an underlying informational structure.

From this perspective, the Born rule is not a primary law of nature but a consequence of the interaction between the physical interface and the informational level.

The imaginary unit is denoted by i and satisfies the relation

\[ i^2=-1 \]

Let's consider the complex conjugate number. If \(\psi=i\), then \(\psi^*=-i\).

Multiplying them:

\[ \psi^*\psi=(-i)\cdot i \]

Rearranging the factors, we obtain

\[ -(i\cdot i) \]

But

\[ i\cdot i=i^2=-1 \]

Therefore,

\[ -(-1)=1 \]

For any complex number \( z=a+bi \), its complex conjugate has the form \( z^*=a-bi \). Their product is:

\[ z^*z=(a-bi)(a+bi) \]

Expanding the brackets, we obtain:

\[ z^*z=a^2+abi-abi-b^2i^2 \]

Since \(i^2=-1\), we have:

\[ z^*z=a^2+b^2 \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{z^*z=a^2+b^2\geq0} \]

For the wave function \(\Psi=Ae^{i\phi}\) the complex conjugate function has the form:

\[ \Psi^*=Ae^{-i\phi} \]

Therefore:

\[ |\Psi|^2=\Psi^*\Psi = Ae^{-i\phi}Ae^{i\phi} \]

We obtain:

\[ |\Psi|^2 = A^2e^{-i\phi}e^{i\phi} = A^2 \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{|\Psi|^2=A^2} \]

the observable probability is obtained.

If the Information Qubit continuously oscillates between the informational state and the quantum state, its description naturally gives rise to two mutually orthogonal components. One component corresponds to the informational state, while the other corresponds to the quantum state.
