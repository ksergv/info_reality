What Is Quantum Coherence?

Quantum coherence describes the ability of quantum states to preserve stable phase relationships.

Coherence allows quantum systems to exhibit interference and superposition, making many quantum phenomena possible.

When coherence is lost through interaction with the environment, quantum decoherence occurs and classical behavior emerges.

Modern quantum theory describes coherence mathematically but does not explain its deeper origin.

The information model assumes that quantum coherence reflects the consistency of information states existing in an information field.

The coordinated interactions of information qubits form coherent quantum states. When interacting with the environment, these states undergo quantum decoherence, resulting in the emergence of observable physical reality.

In this interpretation, quantum coherence is the physical expression of a deeper informational order.
